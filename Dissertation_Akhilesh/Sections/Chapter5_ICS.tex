\section*{Abstract}
Energy efficiency in high-performance computing (HPC) systems is increasingly
critical, yet balancing energy savings against application performance remains a
fundamental challenge. Existing reinforcement learning (RL)-based approaches either
require costly online exploration or train separate agents for each desired
performance-energy trade-off, limiting their practicality in production HPC
environments. In this chapter, we present \textit{Decay-Driven Multi-Objective
Reinforcement Learning} (DDMORL), a single offline-trained controller that allows
users to specify their maximum tolerable performance degradation as a percentage,
which is automatically converted to a preference vector aligned with the
corresponding operating point on the Pareto front. This eliminates the need for
multiple agents, manual preference tuning, or online retraining. DDMORL extends
the Preference-Driven MORL (PDMORL) framework to an offline setting by replacing
the conventional preference-alignment interpolator with a lightweight,
application-specific progress-to-PCAP model. The controller is trained on
pre-collected execution traces from a mixed dataset of eight benchmarks, comprising
STREAM memory-bandwidth variants and NAS Parallel Benchmarks. It is then deployed
without retraining on out-of-distribution test workloads, where it produces
well-structured Pareto fronts while keeping performance degradation within the
user-specified limit.
